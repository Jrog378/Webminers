## 1. What
- **Bible Loop** is a free Bible app for young teens and preteens. YouVersion (Life.Church) built it with OneHope. YouVersion announced it as "the newest app in the YouVersion Family of Apps" in a PR Newswire release dated **Nov 4, 2026** (faith-01).
- It is on Android and iOS in English, Spanish, Portuguese and French (faith-02).
- **Features named in the release** (faith-03):
  - verse memorization game with an "anonymous leaderboard"
  - **Learning Loops**, interactive lessons from partner ministries. The release uses future tense ("will add"), but the store notes from Oct 28–29 say Learning Loops are already live (faith-12).
  - **Daily Check-Ins**, which match devotional content to the teen's mood
  - Verse of the Day and Daily Prayers
  - 50+ Bible versions in 10 languages
- **Features named only in the store listings, not the release:**
  - Streaks: "Level up your Streak," "Build your Streak by completing a Bible activity" (faith-13)
  - Crowns: "earn a crown"
  - Personalized profiles: "Create a personalized profile to track your journey and progress" (faith-14). iOS version notes also mention "User Profiles to keep track of progress" and a "Memory Verse Leaderboard."
  - The memory game has four levels. Finishing them shows where you stand on the leaderboard (youversion.com/bible-loop).

## 4. Why it matters
- **The research behind the app is the age dip.** The release cites a OneHope/Barna study in which engaged Christian parents reported weekly Bible engagement of 88% for ages 6–9 and 73% for ages 10–12 (faith-06).
  - **Caveat 1:** these are parent reports from *engaged Christian* families, not all children.
  - **Caveat 2: the year is in doubt.** The release says "2021," but Barna's pages for the same *Guiding Children* study are dated Feb and Apr **2020**. On Barna's April 2020 page, Hoskins describes "a substantial dip in Bible engagement for 10–12-year-olds" (faith-08). Safest wording: "a OneHope study with Barna," with no year.
  - The 88/73 figures themselves appear only in the release. I could not open OneHope's fact-card PDF (404).
- **Screen time:** OneHope says Gen Alpha spends 4 hr 6 min online daily (faith-07). This is a OneHope claim; the methodology is not public.
- **Why it's encouraging:** the app is free with no ads and no in-app purchases (faith.tools, about Google Play). It is aimed at the age when habits slip, and it fits the "meet them where they are" approach.

## 5. What's contested / the counterpoint
**a) Gamification (the main counterpoint, from someone other than the makers).**
- A 2026 arXiv preprint by Price, Blake, Prather and Poulsen (Utah State and Abilene Christian) surveyed 407 students. It summarizes a peer-reviewed CHI '26 study (Smith et al., "Understanding Digital Religion in the Lives of Black Christian Young Adults").
- Smith et al. found that a Bible app's gamified "streak" "made some participants anxious and felt like an inauthentic way to track something as personal as scripture reading." The streak also "broke whenever a participant chose to read a physical Bible instead of the app" (faith-23).
- The preprint authors conclude that "designers should be wary of adding engagement-driving features, such as streaks ... that treat scripture study as a metric to optimize rather than a personal practice" (faith-24).
- Status:
  - the arXiv paper is a preprint
  - the CHI '26 finding is peer-reviewed, but I read it only as quoted in the preprint
  - the streak finding concerns an unnamed Bible app (YouVersion was the most-used app in that sample), not Bible Loop
- **Balance:** the same preprint notes that apps built around scripture content saw their most-used features there (faith-25). Its own survey found paper Bibles still widely used "especially when the reader is seeking a spiritual experience" (faith-26).
- **A teen reviewer on the App Store made the same point in her own words:** "While reading the physical Bible and praying your own words are always best, this app is a great way to quickly connect with Him." (faith-27)
- **Precision for the writer:** the release itself mentions only the *anonymous* leaderboard. Streaks and crowns come from the store listings.

**b) Privacy and data for minors.** I checked the reviewer's flag against the store disclosures and the privacy policy. It is partly supported but needs careful wording. All of the disclosures below are self-declared by the developer.
- **Google Play Data safety** (faith-17, faith-18):
  - "No data shared with third parties"
  - data collected: device or other IDs, app interactions, approximate location (optional), crash logs and diagnostics, all marked "Analytics"
  - **"Data can't be deleted: The developer doesn't provide a way for you to request that your data be deleted."**
- **Apple privacy label** (faith-19): "Data Not Linked to You," covering identifiers (device ID), usage data and user content (photos or videos). Apple notes this "has not been verified by Apple."
- **The app does have profiles.** iOS advertises "personalized profile[s]" (faith-14).
- **How accounts and profiles work is unclear.** faith.tools, citing YouVersion help, says there is no account creation and progress stays on the device (faith-22). The help page returned 403, so I could not read it myself.
- **Privacy policy:**
  - Bible Loop links to the general policy at bible.com/privacy (last modified Nov 16, 2025). The policy lists Bible Loop among covered apps.
  - It says "You do not need to sign up for an account" (faith-20).
  - It handles minors generically, through parental consent and the line "You are solely responsible for providing supervision of the minor's use of YouVersion" (faith-21).
  - It has no section specific to Bible Loop or teens. "Age 13" and "COPPA" do not appear (per WebFetch extraction).
- **Safe wording:** "Store disclosures say the app collects analytics data that is not linked to a user's identity and not shared with third parties. Google Play also says there's no way to request deletion. YouVersion's general privacy policy covers the app but says little specifically about teens, so parents should read it."
- **Do NOT write** "collects profiles from minors" as fact.
