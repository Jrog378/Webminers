# Dossier: faith section, week of Oct 4-10, 2026

**Feature:** Anxiety, hope and healing: what people searched for in the Bible App this year (YouVersion, Oct 6, 2026)

**Scope applied:** technology only. Worldwide figures and search themes only. Left out of this dossier and the claims file: regional rankings and growth, regional hubs, city or public-space activations, and the persecution framing in the Envoy Mission description.

## 1. What
- YouVersion's annual data release reports 18.5 million worldwide daily users, up "over 13%" from 2025 [f1, f2].
- The app added more than 160M installs in 2026, after passing 1 billion installs in November 2025 [f3].
- "Anxiety," "hope" and "healing" were among the most-searched terms of the year. "Psalm 91" was the single most-searched term [f4].
- The YouVersion Family of Apps offers 3,800 Bible versions in 2,400 languages, with a network of more than 45,000 churches and partners [f5].
- The release previews Global Bible Month (November) and its 30-Day Bible Challenge. The Challenge works with "a favorite print Bible or digitally through an app" [f8]. Nearly 3M people signed up in 2025. The new feature this year: "track their progress and see how their friends are engaging alongside them" [f6].

## 2. How
- The figures are YouVersion's own aggregate analytics. Its privacy policy says it collects "search history" [f13] and publishes engagement stats only "in an aggregated form" [f15].
- **Methodology gap:** the release does not define "daily use." It could mean average daily active users or something else. It also does not say how search terms were counted, for example raw queries or deduplicated ones. Treat the numbers as company-reported and unaudited.

## 3. Who
- YouVersion is "a digital ministry of Life.Church" and its apps are free [f9].
- Bobby Gruenewald is founder and CEO [f7].
- The release went out on PR Newswire and was mirrored on Webull (the same text, so it is not an independent source).

## 4. Why it matters
- This is rare large-scale behavioral data on what people bring to Scripture. The 2026 themes (anxiety, hope, healing, Psalm 91 as refuge) continue the pattern from 2025, when the top searches were "love, anxiety, and peace" and Isaiah 41:10 ("do not fear") was the top verse [f10].
- Writer angle: two years in a row, the app's data shows people opening the Word in their fears. The tool is meeting real need.

## 5. What's contested / limitations
- **Need is not transformation.** A search or a streak measures someone opening the Bible, not discipleship. The 2025 release itself pivots on a single-day peak of "more than 19 million people opening the app" [f12]. Opening an app is the unit of measurement.
- **Metrics shift between releases.** In 2025 YouVersion said "more than 2.6 million people took" the Challenge [f11]. The 2026 release says "nearly 3 million people worldwide signed up" for the same 2025 Challenge [f6]. "Took" and "signed up" are different measures, so do not compare them directly.
- **Persuasive design.** Sara-Jo Swiatek (UChicago Divinity School Sightings, 2013) argued that YouVersion pairs Scripture with "the same methods advertisers use" [f16]. This is old but independent. The new friend-progress feature is a social-comparison mechanic of the same kind, so the critique applies.
- **Sensitive data.** The app logs what people search in their struggles (search history is in the policy [f13]). YouVersion says it does not sell personal data or share it with advertisers [f14] and publishes only aggregates [f15]. That is a company statement, not an audit.
- **AI-generated Scripture media (wisdomConcern).** On the same day (Oct 6), Pray.com launched Pray Production Studio, which turns a sermon into AI cinematic video starting at $19/month [f26]. Scholars told NPR about the related AI Bible series that it "situates the Bible as entertainment, as content to be titillated or amused by" (Jeffrey Bilbro, Grove City College) [f27]. Brad East said short-form viral content "robs it of its power" [f28].
- **Strongest counterpoint (non-author):** Bilbro's line [f27]. It names the risk that the Word becomes content rather than "a word that is a divine revelation intended to transform our lives."

## 6. What's next

- YouVersion usually announces its Verse of the Year in early December. The 2025 announcement was dated Dec 4, 2025 [f10]. Expect a follow-up release with Challenge totals.

**Ascension Catholic Reading Bible: Dyslexia-Friendly Edition (released Oct 8 for International Dyslexia Day; RNS feed item posted Oct 9)**
- Source type: this is Ascension's own press release, carried by the "RNS Press Release Distribution Service." It is not RNS reporting, so attribute it to Ascension. The article page was behind Cloudflare. The full text was read from the RNS RSS feed.
- Ascension calls it "the first complete Catholic Bible to feature the Grace typeface" [f17].
- Grace was developed by 2K/DENMARK and informed by "testing with dyslexic readers, and work involving the University of Cambridge" [f18].
- Design: weighted letter bases, lines of no more than 35 characters, ragged-right text, 11-pt type [f19].
- RSV-2CE translation, $45, with nine colored overlays and one gray overlay [f20]. CEO Jonathan Strate cites Dei Verbum on "easy access to Sacred Scripture" [f21].
- Context: Grace was first used by Lifeway (CSB) and Crossway (ESV) in 2024. Designer Klaus Erik Krogh made it nonproprietary [f22].
- Caveat: "research-tested" here means design testing by 2K/DENMARK with Cambridge involvement. I found no published peer-reviewed results for Grace. Independent studies of earlier dyslexia fonts (OpenDyslexic, Dyslexie) found no gain in speed or accuracy [f23]. Grace differs from those fonts because it bundles layout principles (line length, spacing), so the evidence does not transfer directly. Suggested wording: "designed with input from dyslexic readers and Cambridge researchers," not "proven to help."
