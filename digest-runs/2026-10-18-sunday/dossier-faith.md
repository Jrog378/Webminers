# Dossier: faith — "AI is setting the whole Bible to music, word for word"

Run: 2026-10-18-sunday (month review, Sept 17 to Oct 17, 2026). Claim IDs (f#) refer to `claims-faith.json`. Everything below comes from pages fetched on 2026-10-17. None of this is research; all reach and accuracy figures are claimed by the organizations themselves.

## 1. What
Two separate teams have used AI to produce sung recordings of every chapter of the Bible. Both say they sing the text word for word, with no paraphrase and no added choruses.

## 2. How
- **Music Bible's pledge** (AI-transparency page, signed by Fiechter, undated): AI will "never" be used to "interpret, paraphrase, or extrapolate" Scripture (f9). Verses are kept in order with no choruses, **but** "occasionally, a word or phrase may be repeated for musical expression" (f10). The writer should note this small exception to the "unedited" claim. The commitments include "Maintaining at least 100% accuracy in the oral representation of each passage" (odd wording, quote it exactly) (f12). The page describes no QA process and gives no error rate.
- **TheSinging.Bible**: "every recording follows the written text, which is shown with every chapter" (f22). Read-along text is shown on screen (f20, f27). Answered Faith, citing the RNS-distributed release, says producers, engineers and QC reviewers "remained responsible for managing and reviewing the recordings" (f31).
- **Translation wrinkle (worth checking with the project before printing):** The BSB has been public domain since 2023-04-30. Its terms ask that "derivative works that vary from the official text" not use the Berean name (f37, f38). TheSinging.Bible calls its text the "Singing Bible Edition, based on" the BSB rather than the BSB. That suggests some variation from the BSB, but I could not find what differs. **Unverified.**

## 4. Why it matters
- Both are aimed at listeners and memorizers. Fiechter: "If someone can memorize the lyrics to a pop song, why shouldn't they be able to memorize Scripture the same way?" (Crosswalk). Music Bible says "Two-thirds of the world learns by listening" (f16; company claim, source not given).
- Scale: whole-Bible sung settings in 14 to 22 styles were impractical by hand. Music Bible says the 750,000+ words "exceeded what any single artist or team could accomplish" (f11).
- Church uses: TheSinging.Bible publishes "23 Ways Churches Can Use" it: pre-service Scripture, sermon text of the week, hospital/homebound ministry, new-believer listening path (thesinging.bible/23-ways).
- Context: Barna (10-06) found only **33%** of pastors feel positive about AI writing worship songs and 29% about AI interpreting scripture, versus 64% for AI-built curriculum (f39). In earlier Barna data (08-25), 94% of pastors are concerned about AI misinterpreting scripture (f42). Nuance for the writer: these projects claim AI writes only the *music*, not the lyrics, so they fall in between Barna's categories. Barna did not ask about sung Scripture specifically. Survey: n=442 Protestant pastors, Dec 2025, +/-2.8% (f41).

## 5. What's contested (counterpoints, from people other than the founders)
1. **No published accuracy or error rates.** Answered Faith: the release "does not identify all the tools used, explain how synthetic voices were generated or publish detailed error rates" (f32). Music Bible's page also gives no QA numbers (f12).
2. **Melody interprets.** "A musical performance is not a new form of biblical authority. Melody involves interpretation." A playful style can clash with lament, judgment or the Passion. AI can "mispronounce names, place emphasis in strange locations" (f33, f34).
3. **Reach numbers are self-reported.** The play counts are unaudited, and "a play should not be confused with a unique listener or a completed chapter" (f29). "Plays... cannot measure repentance, understanding, obedience" (f35). Music Bible's 5.7B-by-2028 counter is an aspiration.
4. **Human artistry and authenticity.** Worship leader Derek Volkmann (Worship Strategies, Feb 2026), writing about YouVersion's "Bible in a Year Through Song" and saying explicitly that his suspicion of AI production is "observational, not a confirmed claim": "Even though the words are true, it doesn't necessarily feel true." He will "always promote the human alternative" but wouldn't stop anyone listening (f43–f45). Baptist worship pastors quoted by Religion Unplugged/Baptist Press: Jon Huff calls AI songs "Soul-less"; Kevin Uhrich accepts AI in production but says the words "must remain the work of a human" (f46). Scripture songs arguably meet Uhrich's test, since the words are Scripture and not machine-written. Kenny Lamm (NC Baptists): use discernment rather than blanket fear; AI "can assist the craft but cannot replace the heart and witness of human worshippers" (f47).
5. **Numbers don't agree across sources.** Music Bible is given as 30,000+ (Crosswalk), 38,000 (op-ed) and 38,556 (counter). TheSinging.Bible had 16 styles at launch and 22 now. Give both with dates.

**Strongest single counterpoint line:** "Pastors should also make clear that a musical performance is not a new form of biblical authority. Melody involves interpretation." (Duke Taber, Answered Faith, 2026-09-10)

## 6. What's next
- TheSinging.Bible: "more styles are on the way" (funfacts page). Answered Faith reports more languages are in development.
- Music Bible: more Spanish genres "soon to come" (Crosswalk). It hopes human artists will perform "Word for word Scripture Songs" (AI-transparency page). Its newsletter promises "new translations, new genres."
- Open questions for the writer to raise: published error rates or independent audits; which English translation Music Bible uses; how SBE differs from BSB.
